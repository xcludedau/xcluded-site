KNOW WHAT YOU'RE CHOOSING

The details matter.

A little knowledge makes choosing your tint easier. Here's what the numbers mean — and what to ask before deciding.

01 / VISIBLE LIGHT

What does 35%, 20% or 5% mean?

VLT stands for visible light transmission: the percentage of visible light that passes through the glass and film together. A lower percentage means a darker result. The final VLT depends on the existing glass as well as the film.

Darkness affects privacy and outward visibility, particularly at night. The darkest option isn't necessarily the best option for every vehicle.

02 / HEAT & COMFORT

Not all heat figures are equal.

Infrared rejection (IR) describes performance against a particular part of the infrared spectrum. Total solar energy rejected (TSER) considers the sun's energy more broadly, including visible and ultraviolet wavelengths.

When comparing films, check the test method and the specific shade. A headline IR figure alone doesn't tell the whole story.

03 / UV PROTECTION

UV and heat are different.

UV rejection measures how much ultraviolet radiation is blocked under a stated test method. It is not the same measurement as heat rejection or VLT. For the precise performance of a PRIMA shade, refer to the manufacturer's current technical data.

04 / CHOOSING A SHADE

Looks, visibility and legality.

Queensland vehicle tint requirements vary by window position and vehicle circumstances. We discuss suitable options before installation and check applicable requirements rather than assuming every shade is road legal.

A lighter film can still offer solar-control benefits; appearance and performance aren't always tied to darkness.

05 / AFTER INSTALLATION

Give the film time to settle.

Newly installed film can show temporary moisture or haze while it dries. Curing time depends on conditions and the product used. Follow the care instructions provided with your installation, including when it's safe to lower the windows and clean the glass.

GOOD QUESTIONS. CLEAR ANSWERS.

Frequently asked questions.

A few things worth knowing before choosing your finish. Select a question to see the answer.

What does 35%, 20% or 5% tint actually mean?

These numbers refer to visible light transmission (VLT). Lower percentages let less visible light through. The finished window's VLT depends on both the film and the glass already fitted to your vehicle.

Does darker tint mean better heat rejection?

Not necessarily. Darkness and solar performance are different properties. When comparing films, look at shade-specific total solar energy rejected (TSER) figures rather than judging performance by appearance alone.

What's the difference between UV and infrared rejection?

UV rejection describes how much ultraviolet radiation is blocked under a stated test method. Infrared rejection describes performance across a specified infrared range. Neither figure alone tells you the film's total solar heat performance.

Which tint shade is legal in Queensland?

Queensland's requirements depend on the window position and vehicle circumstances, and the limit applies to the finished glass-and-film combination. We'll discuss applicable requirements before installation rather than treating every advertised shade as road legal.

Can I wind down my windows straight after tinting?

Fresh film needs time to cure. Follow the specific aftercare instructions supplied at installation, including when to lower your windows and how to clean the glass. Drying time can vary with weather and the product used.

Will small bubbles or haze disappear?

Some temporary moisture or haziness can be normal during curing. If marks persist beyond the advised curing period, or you notice lifting or contamination, contact us so we can assess them.

What warranty comes with PRIMA film?

ZR Films describes PRIMA as having a limited lifetime warranty against fading, delamination, bubbling and cracking. Eligibility, exclusions, claim procedures and any installation workmanship coverage depend on the written terms supplied for your job; ask XCLUDED to confirm these before booking.
